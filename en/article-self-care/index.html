Self-Care: More Than a Warm Bath and a Cup of Tea

5 min read · Bewoduh team

When we hear "self-care," the first thing that usually comes to mind is a stereotype: a warm bath, a scented candle, a cup of tea. These things aren't wrong, but they're not the whole story. Real self-care runs much deeper, and sometimes it's far less comfortable than we imagine.

Self-care isn't always comfortable

Real self-care sometimes means things that aren't comfortable at all: saying "no" to a request that drains you, ending a toxic relationship or friendship, finally booking the doctor's appointment you've been putting off, or even admitting that you need help. Surface-level self-care is helpful, but it doesn't replace the deeper self-care that requires real decisions.

The dimensions of real self-care

  • Physical care: enough sleep, regular movement, nutrition that supports your energy — don't deprive yourself of these just because you're short on time.
  • Emotional care: allowing yourself to feel what you feel without judgment, and asking for support when you need it.
  • Social care: maintaining relationships that nourish you, and gradually stepping back from relationships that drain you.
  • Practical care: organizing your day-to-day affairs (financial, practical) in a way that reduces ongoing stress instead of ignoring it.
  • Care through boundaries: learning to say no to what doesn't work for you, even if you feel guilty at first.

How do you actually start?

You don't need to change your whole life at once. Start with a simple question you ask yourself daily: "What's the one thing about myself I neglected most today?" — an honest answer to that question, even if it leads to just a small step, is the beginning of real self-care.

Real self-care sometimes looks more like a hard decision than a moment of relaxation.
