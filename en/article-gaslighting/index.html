Gaslighting: Why You Doubt Yourself After Every Argument

8 min read · Bewoduh team

If you've noticed that you've started writing down anything important in a message "just to protect yourself," or that you've begun doubting your own memory about simple things you used to be sure of, you may be under the influence of a well-known manipulation pattern called gaslighting.

What Exactly Is Gaslighting?

Gaslighting is a manipulation pattern that targets one thing specifically: your perception of your own reality. It doesn't necessarily involve yelling or obvious control — most of the time it happens through the repeated denial of things that actually happened, to the point where you start doubting your judgment in general, not just about that specific topic.

— You promised me you'd come at seven.
— I never said that. You always hear what you want to hear.

Why Is It Hard to Notice While You're Inside It?

Because it happens very gradually. One instance of this pattern might pass as an ordinary misunderstanding, but repetition is what builds the real effect: every time you doubt yourself a little more, you become more dependent on the "version of reality" the other person offers, because you've lost trust in your own.

The telling sign isn't the forgetting itself — it's the insistence that you're "wrong or overreacting" even in the face of clear evidence.

Common Phrases Worth Paying Attention To

  • "You're making things up" or "That's not what happened"
  • "You're too sensitive" as a recurring response to any feelings you express
  • "No one told you that, you're imagining it"
  • "Why do you always blow everything out of proportion?"

A single one of these phrases isn't definitive proof — anyone might use one occasionally in an ordinary situation. What matters here is the repetition and the pattern, not the isolated sentence.

A Practical Step You Can Start Today

Document. We don't mean gathering evidence for a confrontation — we mean writing down for yourself what happened and when, in a private journal or your phone's notes app. This helps you tell the difference between "your momentary feeling" and the "real pattern," which only becomes visible once you see the events gathered together over time.
